01 / Point of view

Built by leaving the bureaucracy out.

Most project trackers start out simple, then get redesigned around the person who wants a quarterly slide deck instead of the engineer fixing a webhook timeout or the designer refining an empty state.

Every mandatory dropdown, story-point Fibonacci debate, and nested sub-epic steals fifteen minutes from the actual build. We designed Trestle as a working bench: crisp lists, fast keyboard navigation, and deliberate limits on how much work a cycle can hold.

02 / How it works

Three mechanics that keep a two-week cycle honest.

02.1

Cycles with visible scope creep.

Set a two-week commitment on Monday morning. Whenever someone adds a mid-cycle request, Trestle draws a thin vermilion tick on the scope line so the team immediately sees the trade-off—not two weeks later during a retrospective.

Tracks planned vs. added items · Zero burndown shaming
CYCLE 18 SCOPE LEDGER 10 PLANNED · +2 ADDED
02.2

Decisions stay pinned to the work.

Six months from now, nobody will find the Slack thread explaining why you dropped Redis for Postgres advisory locks. Trestle gives every project a lightweight Decisions ledger that cross-links directly to task IDs and pull requests.

Markdown-native · Permanent URLs · Searchable in <40ms
DEC-042 · ACCEPTED Nov 19 · @mira
Use SQLite WAL snapshots instead of daily CSV dumps for workspace exports

Customers asked for lossless offline backups including decision history. Shipping a single .sqlite file takes 140ms on median workspaces and avoids fragile multi-file ZIP schemas.

Linked: TR-114 PR #819 merged
02.3

Friday digests that write themselves—literally.

At 4:00 PM Friday, Trestle compiles a clean summary from shipped tasks, resolved blockers, and newly recorded decisions. No hallucinated AI fluff—just structured facts you can paste into an investor update or send to clients.
