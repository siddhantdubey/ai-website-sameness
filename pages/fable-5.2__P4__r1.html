§ 1

What we handle

Three areas, chosen on purpose. We'd rather know them thoroughly than take every matter that comes through the door.

  1. 1.1

    Small business

    Setting a company up properly, then keeping its paperwork from becoming a problem later. Most of our business clients have fewer than thirty employees.

    LLC and corporation formation · operating agreements · commercial leases · vendor and customer contracts · buying or selling a business

  2. 1.2

    Employment

    We represent both employees and small employers, never in the same matter. Often the most useful hour is the one before you sign something.

    Severance and non-compete review · unpaid wages · discrimination and retaliation claims · handbooks and policies · workplace investigations

  3. 1.3

    Wills and estates

    Plans a family can actually follow, and steady help with probate when someone has died. We meet at your kitchen table if that's easier.

    Wills · powers of attorney · health care directives · simple trusts · probate administration

We don't take criminal, immigration, or personal injury cases. If that's what you need, call anyway and we'll give you the names of lawyers we trust.

§ 2

How we work

The same four steps for every client, whether the matter takes an afternoon or a year.

  1. A short call, freeTwenty minutes with a lawyer. We'll tell you whether you need one at all, and whether we're the right ones.
  2. Scope and fee, in writingA one-page engagement letter says what we'll do, what it costs, and what would change that number. Nothing starts until you've signed it.
  3. The work, with updatesYou hear from us whenever something happens and every two weeks when nothing has. You'll always know what the next step is and who is taking it.
  4. A clean finishYou receive your complete file, a plain-language summary of where things stand, and a note of any dates to keep on your calendar.

§ 5

Questions people ask first

Do I actually need a lawyer for this?

Sometimes not. If a form from the Secretary of State or a call to the Department of Labor will solve it, we'll say so on the first call and point you to the right page.

What should I have ready for the first call?

The document at the center of things, if there is one (the contract, the letter, the offer), and any deadline you've been given. Don't send anything in advance; we'll ask for it once we've confirmed we can represent you.

Can we meet in person?

Yes. The office is on the second floor at 48 Exchange Street, with an elevator from the lobby. We also meet by video, by phone, and for estate clients, at home.

What if my matter ends up in court?

We appear regularly in Maine's District, Superior, and Probate courts. If your matter needs a specialist or a larger team, we'll tell you early and help you move it.
