You describe the work. Plumb does the calendar.

Tasks, estimates, and what’s waiting on what. That’s the whole input.

  • Float is shown, not hidden

    Every task shows how many days it can slip before the finish date moves. People stop padding estimates when everyone can see where the padding is.

  • Time off counts

    Connect a calendar and holidays, vacations and that offsite in week three are already in the math. No one has to remember to mention them.

  • The original plan stays put

    The first schedule you commit to is drawn under every bar from then on. Nobody can quietly edit history to make the project look on time.

  • One email on Monday

    The finish date, whether it moved last week, and what moved it. Three lines, sent to whoever keeps asking.

  • Keep your issue tracker

    Two-way sync with Linear, Jira and GitHub Issues. Close an issue over there and the bar closes here. Engineers never have to open Plumb.

  • Share a link, not a login

    Clients and stakeholders get a read-only page with the current date and the reasons behind it. Viewers are always free.

Plumb is for projects that end: launches, migrations, builds, client work with a date in the contract. If your work is a queue of tickets that never empties, a kanban board will serve you better, and we’d rather you used one.

The date has a history.

Every time the finish date moves, Plumb writes down what moved it. When someone asks in January why launch slipped, you don’t reconstruct it from chat logs. You read it.

Fair questions

Is this a Gantt chart?

Yes. Gantt charts earned their bad name because someone drew them by hand in week one and they were wrong by week two. This one redraws itself whenever the work changes, which is the only version of the idea that was ever useful.

Our estimates are always wrong. Does that break it?

Everyone’s are. Plumb keeps track of how each estimate compared with what happened, per person and per kind of task, and can widen the projected finish into a range based on your own record. You get a more honest date without anyone having to guess better.

Can I bring in a project from a spreadsheet?

Paste the rows or upload a CSV. Plumb needs a task name and an estimate; owner and “waiting on” columns are picked up if they’re there. Most people have a first schedule on screen in a few minutes.

Do you do sprints, story points or velocity?

No. Plumb plans in days and dates because that’s what the people outside your team are asking about. If your engineers run sprints in another tool, the sync keeps both sides current.

What happens to my data if I leave?

Export everything, including the date history, as CSV or JSON at any time on either plan. Close your account and we delete it within 30 days.
