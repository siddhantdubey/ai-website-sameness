How it works

Built around the week, because that's how people actually plan.

01 — Monday

Everyone writes down what they'll get done.

A short list per person, grouped by project. Takes about four minutes. Plover nudges in Slack if someone forgets.

02 — During the week

Tick things off. Leave a line if you're stuck.

No statuses to pick from, no columns to drag between. Done, not done, or blocked with a sentence saying why.

03 — Friday

A digest goes out. Leftovers roll forward.

Anything that's rolled over twice gets flagged, so the "we'll get to it" items stop hiding at the bottom.

What it won't do

We said no to a lot of features. On purpose.

  • Story points. You'll estimate wrong either way. Plover tracks what got done, not how many points it was worth.
  • Gantt charts. If you need dependencies across 40 workstreams, you've outgrown us, and that's fine.
  • Custom fields. Every task has a title, an owner, a project, and an optional note. Turns out that covers almost everything.
  • An AI that writes your tasks. You know what you're doing this week better than we do.

We moved eleven people off Jira in an afternoon. Our Monday meeting went from fifty minutes to twelve, and nobody's asked to go back.
