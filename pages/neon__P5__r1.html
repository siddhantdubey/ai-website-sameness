01 / Philosophy

Most project trackers turn shipping software into data entry.

When a tool encourages 12 custom workflow columns, sub-sub-tasks, and an unbounded backlog, teams spend Tuesday mornings arguing about ticket hygiene instead of finishing the work. Docket leaves those knobs out on purpose.

01

Six-week cycles, not bottomless backlogs

Work either belongs in this cycle or it stays in a quiet Reading Pile. Anything that sits unscheduled for two cycles archives itself automatically. If an idea truly matters, someone will bring it back up.

02

The spec lives right beside the work

Every scope starts as a plain markdown page explaining what we are building and what we are explicitly cutting. No hunting across three wikis and a Slack thread to figure out why a task exists.

03

Honest timelines over fake velocity

Story points measure how good a team is at inventing numbers. Docket tracks completed work against remaining scopes and calls out mid-cycle scope creep in plain sight before a deadline slips.

Area Typical ticket factory How Docket handles it
Backlog size 600–2,000 stale issues nobody trusts enough to close. Zero backlog. Active cycle + a small, auto-expiring Reading Pile.
Workflow states 11 configurable statuses ("Ready for QA Staging Review"). Four fixed states: Todo, In Progress, In Review, Done.
Status updates 30-minute daily standups plus a Friday slide deck. One calm Friday Digest compiled from shipped work and owner notes.
Client / exec visibility Exporting screenshots because the board is unreadable. Read-only share link for any cycle with clean progress and notes.
02 / Details

Small decisions that keep the tool out of your way.

Built as a fast, local-first web and macOS application with a first-class CLI. No loading spinners between views, no modal traps, and no noisy bot spam in your pull requests.

Scope creep shown in plain daylight

Most progress bars lie by stretching quietly when new tasks get slipped into a sprint. Docket hatches mid-cycle additions separately so the team sees when a scope is growing.

Quiet GitHub & GitLab integration

Mention a task ID in a branch name or PR title and Docket moves it to In Review when opened and Done on merge—without posting six boilerplate bot comments on your PR.
