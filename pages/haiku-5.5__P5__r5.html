How it works

Plans live next to the work, not in a slide deck.

Most project tools ask you to maintain a second copy of reality. Tallyboard reads the state of your tasks and tells you where it has drifted from the plan.

  • 01

    Blockers are first-class

    Mark a task as blocked by another task, a person, or an outside vendor. Blocked items surface at the top of the board and in your daily digest, with how long they have been waiting.

  • 02

    Owners on every card

    Each task has one owner. Reassigning moves the card, notifies the new owner, and updates the workload view so nobody quietly ends up with eleven things open.

  • 03

    Timelines that update themselves

    When a dependency slips, the dates on everything downstream move with it. You see the consequence before you commit to a new date in front of stakeholders.

  • 04

    A digest instead of a meeting

    Every morning, each person gets a short list: what they own, what is waiting on them, and what changed overnight. Standups become a five-minute check of exceptions.

Getting started

Running a project in three steps.

1

Import what you have

Bring in tasks from Jira, Trello, Asana, or a CSV. Statuses and assignees map across automatically, and you can review the mapping before anything is written.

2

Set the dependencies

Draw the links that matter: what must finish before something else can start. Tallyboard calculates the critical path and highlights the tasks that would move the date.

3

Let the digest run

Your team gets a daily summary by email or Slack. Leads open the board only when the digest flags something, which for most teams is a few times a week.
