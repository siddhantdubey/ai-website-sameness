Five opinions

We built it the way we wish our last team had worked.

  1. A cycle has a lid.

    One to six weeks, your call, but it ends on the date it says. You can’t extend it. Work that didn’t finish lands in a Decide pile and someone has to say ship, cut, or defer. Nothing rolls over by default.

  2. One owner per task.

    Co-assignees are how things sit for two weeks while everyone assumes the other person has it. If a task is too big for one person, it’s two tasks.

  3. Start with a page of writing.

    Every project opens with a brief: what, why, what “done” looks like, what we’re not doing. Tasks link back to it. When scope argues with the brief, the brief wins, or gets edited in public.

  4. Estimates are ranges, and we show them.

    “3 to 8 days” is a more honest answer than “5.” Kiln carries the spread into the burndown, so you see a cone of likely finish dates instead of a single line that’s wrong with great confidence.

  5. Quiet by default.

    One digest per person per morning. No red badges, no “someone mentioned you in a thread you’ve already left.” Urgent things can still page you; they have to be marked urgent by a human, with a reason.

Questions

Things people ask before they switch.

What if our work doesn’t fit in cycles?

Support queues and ongoing ops work are better in a plain list, and Kiln has one. Cycles are for anything that’s meant to end: a launch, a migration, a redesign. Most teams run both side by side.

Can we really not extend a cycle?

Correct. You can end it early, or start the next one the following day with the same scope, but the act of closing and re-deciding is the whole point. If that sounds annoying, you may be the team that needs it most.

How do we move over from our current tool?

Import from CSV, Linear, Trello or Jira. Open work comes across as a backlog; closed work comes across as history. Most imports take under ten minutes, and nothing is deleted from the source.

Where does our data live?

Encrypted at rest in the EU or the US, your choice on Studio. Export everything as JSON or Markdown at any time, on any plan.
