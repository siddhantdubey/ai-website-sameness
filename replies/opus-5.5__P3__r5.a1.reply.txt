The portfolio site is saved as **`index.html`**, a single 38 KB file with no external fonts, images or scripts. The JavaScript passes a syntax check, but I haven't opened it in a browser, so the layout and interactions are untested.

**What's on the page**
- **Hero:** a full-screen image with a slow zoom, the headline "Light, slowly observed.", and a caption with shot details. The header turns solid once you scroll past it.
- **Portfolio:** 15 images in a masonry grid with category filters (All / Landscape / Portrait / Urban / Editorial) and counts on each filter. Tiles fade in as you scroll and show the title and year on hover.
- **Lightbox:** previous/next buttons, arrow keys, Escape to close, swipe on phones, and a position counter. Moving between images stays within the current filter. Keyboard focus stays inside the viewer while it's open.
- **About:** a self-portrait, a short bio, three stats and a client list.
- **Services:** four rate rows (portraits, editorial, brand and hospitality, prints).
- **Testimonial**, then **contact** with a checked enquiry form and the studio details.
- **Mobile:** a full-screen menu, the current section highlighted in the nav, and reduced motion for visitors who ask for it.

**Placeholder content you'll want to replace**
- **Photos:** there are no photo files. The landscapes, seas, cities, dunes, forests and portraits are drawn in the browser so the file works offline. To use real photos, add `src: "photos/xyz.jpg"` to an entry in the `PHOTOS` array and it will be used instead of the drawing. Each entry also holds the title, location, year and camera details.
- **Name and text:** "Ines Varga", the bio, clients, prices, testimonial and links are made up.
- **Email:** the contact address is the placeholder `studio@inesvarga.example`.

**Contact form:** there's no server behind it. When someone submits it, their own email app opens with a pre-filled message. To receive submissions directly, point the form at a service such as Formspree or Netlify Forms.
