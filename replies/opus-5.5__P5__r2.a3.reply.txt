          <p class="eyebrow">How a fortnight goes</p>
          <h2>Ten minutes on Monday. Barely anything after.</h2>
          <p>We built Fortnight after years of maintaining boards that nobody looked at past Tuesday. The fix wasn't more features — it was a shorter horizon and fewer places to put things.</p>
        </div>
        <ol class="week">
          <li>
            <time>Mon, 9:40</time>
            <div>
              <h3>Lay out the ten days</h3>
              <p>Drag work onto the timeline and put a face on each bar. Anything that won't fit goes to the Later pile — out of sight, not lost. If someone's booked past ten days, their row goes amber.</p>
            </div>
          </li>
          <li>
            <time>Every day</time>
            <div>
              <h3>Tick things off. That's the update.</h3>
              <p>No stand-up notes, no status fields. A bar that runs past its end date turns red and the owner gets one quiet nudge — not a stream of notifications. Press <kbd>T</kbd> to jump to today.</p>
            </div>
          </li>
          <li>
            <time>Wed, 14:00</time>
            <div>
              <h3>Move things when reality shows up</h3>
              <p>A client calls, a bug lands. Drag the bar, and Fortnight shows who that pushes into overtime before you let go. Plans are supposed to change; they just shouldn't change silently.</p>
            </div>
          </li>
          <li>
            <time>Fri, wk 2</time>
            <div>
              <h3>Close it out in one screen</h3>
              <p>What shipped, what rolled over, what got dropped. Unfinished bars carry into the next fortnight with a small tally, so you notice the task that's been "almost done" for six weeks.</p>
            </div>
          </li>
        </ol>

      <h2>Most of what makes project tools tiring.</h2>
      <p class="intro">People ask for these every month. We've said no every month. If you need them, honestly, there are good tools that have them — Fortnight isn't trying to be those.</p>

        <li><s>Gantt charts with dependencies</s><span>Ten days doesn't need a critical-path diagram. You can see the whole thing.</span></li>
        <li><s>Story points &amp; velocity</s><span>We count days, because that's what people actually have.</span></li>
        <li><s>Custom fields, 14 statuses</s><span>A task is not started, in progress, or done. And sometimes late.</span></li>
        <li><s>Twelve-month roadmaps</s><span>There's a Later pile. It's a list. That's deliberate.</span></li>
        <li><s>Time tracking</s><span>We'd rather you didn't feel watched. Export to Harvest if you bill hourly.</span></li>

**The design choices meant to keep it from looking generated:**
- **Concept:** The product is "Fortnight," a planner with a clear opinion: plan only two weeks ahead. The copy follows from that opinion instead of generic lines like "supercharge your workflow."
- **Look:** Warm paper background, black ink and a single brick-red accent. It uses a serif for headings and a monospace for small labels. There are no gradients, glass effects, emoji icons or a three-card feature grid.
- **Product mockup:** The hero shows a working timeline instead of a stock screenshot. It has specific tasks, a "today" line on Mon Nov 24 and one late task. Ticking a box marks the task done and updates the counters.
