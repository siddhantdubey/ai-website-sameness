      <div class="eyebrow"><span class="n">01</span><span>How it works</span></div>
      <h2>Three rules. We’re strict about them so you don’t have to be.</h2>
      <p class="muted" style="max-width:36em">Most project tools let you build anything, which is how you end up with 400 tasks and no idea what’s actually behind. Foreman has opinions.</p>

      <ol class="rules-list">
        <li>
          <div class="num">i.</div>
          <div><h3>One owner per project. Not a team, a person.</h3><p>Shared ownership means nobody chases the printer. Foreman won’t let you save a project without a name in the owner field.</p></div>
          <div class="aside">You can hand a project off, and the handoff is logged — so “I thought you had it” becomes a thing you can look up.</div>
        </li>
        <li>
          <div class="num">ii.</div>
          <div><h3>Dates move with the work.</h3><p>Link steps together and a slip in one pushes everything after it. The project’s due date stops being a wish and starts being a forecast.</p></div>
          <div class="aside">The original date never disappears. Every project keeps its baseline, so you can see <code>Dec 12 → 15</code> and exactly which step caused it.</div>
        </li>
        <li>
          <div class="num">iii.</div>
          <div><h3>Updates happen where the work is.</h3><p>Comment on a step, attach the file, tick it off. That’s the status update. Nobody writes a separate report.</p></div>
          <div class="aside">If a step goes a week with no activity, its owner gets one quiet nudge. Not a notification storm — one.</div>
        </li>
      </ol>
    </div>
  </section>

- **Look:** warm paper background, a serif headline, monospace labels, thin ruled dividers and one rust-orange accent. There are no purple gradients, frosted glass, emoji icons or grids of feature cards.
- **Product mockup:** the hero shows a project page built in HTML instead of an abstract illustration. It has real-sounding steps and people, a "3 days late" warning explaining why, and a yellow sticky note from a teammate.
- **Copy:** concrete instead of vague. The headline is "Find out it's late on *Tuesday*, not Friday." It names specific competitors and tools, shows a sample Monday digest email, and lists what the product deliberately leaves out.
- **Small human details:** an odd number of plans, notes about mid-month seat billing and nonprofit discounts, a changelog fix that says "Sorry, Auckland," and "Jobs (1 open)" in the footer.
